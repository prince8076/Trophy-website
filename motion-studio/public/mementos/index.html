What a memento is for

A trophy marks a win. A memento marks a moment — a farewell, a retirement, a visit, a ceremony, a programme completed. It is given to someone who mattered to the occasion, not necessarily to someone who came first. That changes how it should be designed: the wording matters more than the size, and the piece should feel personal rather than competitive.

Mementos are most often given at official functions, community programmes, farewells and ceremonies. The recipient might be a guest of honour, a retiring colleague, a volunteer, a speaker, or a partner organisation. The piece carries the name of the event, the date, and often a line that says what the person contributed. Getting that wording right is the heart of the work.

Choosing a memento

Crystal suits the most formal mementos — a farewell for a senior official, a retirement after long service, a ceremony with a chief guest. Wood suits a warmer, more personal farewell, and it takes engraving beautifully. Acrylic suits a community programme or a cultural event, where the memento can carry a shape or a colour that matches the occasion. Metal suits a formal presentation where a salver or a shield is the right form.

The size is usually modest. A memento is a keepsake, not a monument — it should sit comfortably on a desk or a shelf, and be easy to carry home. The engraving is what carries the weight: the person's name, the occasion, the date, and a line that will still mean something in ten years.

The wording matters most

For a memento, spend the most time on the words. A trophy's wording is usually a title and a name. A memento's wording is a small piece of writing — a thank you, a recognition of service, a line about what the person gave. Send us the wording as you want it to appear, and we will set it out for your approval. If you are not sure what to write, tell us about the person and the occasion, and we will help you shape it.

Because mementos are often needed in small numbers — sometimes just one — the design conversation is personal from the start. There is no minimum order, and a single memento gets the same care as a set of fifty.
