What makes a corporate award work

A corporate award has to do two things at once. It has to honour the individual — the salesperson who exceeded target, the engineer who shipped the impossible, the employee who completed twenty-five years. And it has to carry the company — its name, its branding, its standard. A generic trophy with a name stuck on it does neither well. A piece designed around the company's branding, in a material that fits the level of the award, does both.

That is why the design conversation starts with the programme, not with a catalogue. Is this a single lifetime achievement award, or a set of fifty for a sales conference? Is it the top honour of the night, or one of several categories? The answers decide the material, the size and the shape — crystal for the senior recognition, a consistent set in one material for the wider programme, with the company branding carried across every piece.

Branding, done properly

Company branding on a trophy is not a sticker. The logo is laser-engraved into the material, along with the award title and the recipient's name, and the layout is set out for your approval before it is cut. The engraving is included with every piece. For a set, the same layout runs across all of them, so the awards look like a programme rather than a collection of one-offs.

Send the logo as a clear file, along with the award titles and the list of recipients. If the list is not final yet, the design and the material can be settled first and the names added later — that is the normal way a recognition programme is ordered.

Service milestones and long service

Long-service awards deserve particular care. Twenty-five years with a company is not a routine event, and the trophy should not look routine either. Crystal and wood suit these occasions best — crystal for its formality, wood for its warmth. The engraving carries the years of service, the name, and often a line that says what the person meant to the company. That wording is worth getting right, and it is part of the design conversation.

For annual recognition nights, the same care applies at scale. A consistent set in one material, with the company branding and a clear hierarchy — the top award visibly the top award — is what makes the evening feel considered rather than assembled.
