What a trophy manufacturer actually does

A trophy manufacturer is not a shop that sells ready-made cups off a shelf. The work starts with the occasion and the person being recognised, and ends with a piece made for that moment. At Shreeji Corporates the process is consultation first: what is being celebrated, who is receiving it, how many are needed, and when the event is. From that brief comes a design — a shape, a material, a size — and the engraving that makes it personal. Only then does production begin.

Because everything is made to order, there is no catalogue of fixed prices. A small acrylic star for a school sports day and a tall crystal tower for a corporate lifetime achievement award are different pieces of work, and they are quoted as such. What is the same in every order is the standard: the piece is checked before it leaves, packed to arrive unbroken, and engraved precisely.

The studio works in four materials — crystal, metal, acrylic and wood — and each behaves differently. Crystal is heavy, clear and formal; it suits milestones and senior recognition. Metal is the traditional cup and salver, the shape most people picture when they hear the word trophy. Acrylic is light and modern, and it takes colour and unusual shapes well. Wood is warm and understated, and it suits tributes and long-service pieces. A good manufacturer will steer you toward the material that fits the occasion, not just the one you first thought of.

Custom trophies, not off-the-shelf

Customisation is the whole point of a manufacturer. The shape, the theme, the style, the size and the colour are all tailored to the order. A cricket tournament wants a cup; a school annual day wants a mix of cups, shields and medals; a corporate awards night wants a consistent set in one material with the company branding carried across every piece. The engraving is part of the same conversation — the recipient's name, the award title, the date, a logo or a motif, laser-engraved and included with every trophy.

That is why the first message matters more than a catalogue. When you write on WhatsApp, include the occasion, roughly how many pieces you need, and the date of the event. A photo of a trophy you liked, or a logo file, helps the design conversation start faster. If you are not sure what you want, say that too — suggesting shapes and materials for an occasion is part of the job.
