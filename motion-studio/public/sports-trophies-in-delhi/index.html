Complete sets for tournaments and leagues

A sports event rarely needs one trophy. It needs a set: the winner's cup, the runners-up pieces, trophies or medals for every category and age group, and often a player-of-the-match or player-of-the-series award on top. When those pieces come from one maker, the engraving style, the event name and the logo run consistently across all of them, and the set reads as one event rather than a collection of unrelated pieces.

That consistency is planned at the start. Send the event name, the list of categories and the date of the final, and the whole set is designed together — the cup at the top, the medals covering the field, and the same wording standard across every piece.

Cups, trophies and medals that belong together

Metal cups carry the tradition of the occasion — the big final, the photograph, the trophy lifted at the end. Acrylic suits larger numbers and modern formats, where every category winner needs a piece that still feels like an award. Medals cover the wider field: every finalist, every age group, every participant who earned one. Most tournaments use all three, and they should be designed as one family.

The engraving is what ties them together — the event name, the category, the year, and the winner's name where it is known in advance. Where winners are decided on the day, the design can be approved with the names left to be confirmed as late as the event allows.

Planning around a fixed date

A tournament date does not move, and that is what makes sports orders different. The trophies have to be ready for the presentation, not after it. The way to protect the date is to settle the design early: the event name, the logo, the category list and the approximate numbers can all be agreed before the fixtures are finished, and the final counts confirmed closer to the day.

Get in touch as soon as the date is fixed, even if the categories are still being worked out. An approved design early on is what makes a calm delivery later.
